Notes on running a small RSS service

By A. Writer, 3 October 2026

For two years I have run a small service that turns a ranked list of links into an RSS feed with a short summary under each entry. It refreshes once an hour, keeps everything in a single JSON file, and has never needed a database.

The hardest part was not the summaries. It was deciding what to leave out. A list that changes every hour produces far more entries than anyone wants to read, so the service only summarises a story once it has collected enough points to show that people care about it.

Keeping the cache bounded

The cache is bounded twice. A time bound drops entries that left the list more than three days ago, and a size bound evicts the oldest summaries first if the file ever grows past its cap. Entries still on the list are never evicted.

Because a summary is written once and kept, a restart costs nothing. Deleting the file is the only way to force everything to be summarised again, and even then a per-cycle cap spreads the work over several hours.

Bound everything, and make the bounds visible.

The status page reports how many entries were pruned, how many were evicted, and how many were held back by the points gate, so it is easy to tell a quiet day from a broken one.
